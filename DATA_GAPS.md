# Data gaps and cuts

## Lane A (data)
- **Bournemouth (BCP) has no FixMyStreet data.** BCP Council (MapIt area 152188) does not take FixMyStreet reports: every report there shows "Not reported to council", and the Open311 API returns nothing for that agency (checked 17 Oct 2026). Dropped from all lanes.
- **Southampton labels only from 2015.** 2011–14 came through the council's old FixMyStreet-branded site, which inflates counts. 2020–2022 has no reports at all. Usable window 2015–2019 (`config.yaml: labels.windows`). Train 2015–17, test 2018–19.
- **Brighton road reports fall away from mid-2025.** Only one "Pavement and road repairs" report in May–June 2026. Usable window to 2025. Test 2023–25.
- **2026 is partial (Jan–Oct) and small** (Southampton 35, Portsmouth 46, Brighton 32 road-surface reports). It is a "frozen model" check only, reported with n and a bootstrap range.
- **Portsmouth is small** (321 labels after cleaning). It is extra training data / a transfer test, not a headline result.
- **Counts measure FixMyStreet use, not pothole rates.** Scores rank segments within each city and year. Raw totals are never compared across cities.
- **Pavement filter is keyword-based.** A hand check of 25 Brighton reports each way found about 24/25 kept reports were genuine road defects and 22/25 removed ones genuine pavement defects. Some error stays in the labels.
- **Labels cover all road classes** (decided 17 Oct). Only 27% of Southampton 2015–19 reports are on A/B roads, so main-road-only labels would drop most of them. Reports more than 25 m from any drivable road (about 2%) are dropped as off-road/pavement.
- **Most segments have no traffic count of their own.** In Southampton 314 segments use a DfT count point on the same road (≤100 m) and 604 the nearest point on that road. The other 7,227 (89%, mostly residential, tertiary and unreferenced B-roads) get a class median (`load_source=class_median`). Main roads with a real count or same-road value: 44%.
- **ESAL factors are assumptions** (bus 1.3, rigid 2-axle 0.4 … 6-axle artic 2.2 standard axles). They are not from a council or DMRB, and are only used to rank load.
- **Flood = EA Flood Zone 3 only (rivers and sea, Flood Map for Planning, OGL).** It doesn't cover surface-water flooding, which matters for potholes but is a separate EA layer. `in_flood_area` = segment midpoint inside a Zone 3 polygon (Southampton 245 segments, 3%).
- **Junction = 4+ streets or a mini-roundabout/signal node.** In OSM's simplified graph almost every edge ends at a 3-way meet, so those are not counted (16% of Southampton segments are junctions).
- **Portsmouth wards are ONS (Dec 2024), not MapIt.** MapIt's free quota ran out (HTTP 429), so `roadsense.ingest.osm` falls back to the ONS Open Geography Portal. Ward names are the official ones (checked: ONS and MapIt give identical names for Southampton). `mapit_id` is null for Portsmouth.
- **Traffic counts by city:** class_median share is Brighton 84% and Portsmouth 82% of segments (Southampton 89%). Brighton's B-road (secondary) median comes out above its A-road (primary) median because of a few busy counted B-roads. It's left as measured.
- **National Highways roads = motorways only** (10 Southampton segments, M27/M271; 131 Portsmouth, M275/M27). OSM doesn't say who owns trunk A-roads.
- **B-roads often lack a `ref` in OSM** (64 km of Southampton main road has no ref). They are still `is_main_road` by highway class.

## Lane B (models)
- **Lane B outputs built from lane A's branch before Merge 1 (17 Oct, 14:50).** Synthetic data, risk and lights were regenerated on lane A's real Southampton segments, load and Open-Meteo weather, read from `lane-a-data` without merging it. Re-run after Merge 1 so the inputs come from main.
- **Resurfacing age before the recorded date.** Generator 1 records one last_resurfaced date per segment. For weeks before that date, age is assumed to be the class maximum (15/25/40 years) plus the time since, i.e. the segment was on its previous cycle. Assumption, not council data.
- **Pothole labels by segment_id.** Synthetic potholes are generated on a segment and jittered up to 10 m, so labels join on segment_id rather than a 15 m spatial search. FixMyStreet points are snapped to the nearest segment within 15 m.
- **Crew outcomes selection.** Generator 5 picks its 200 top segments from the latest outputs/risk_*.parquet, so run synthetic, then train, then synthetic again.
- **Weather terms capped in the synthetic hazard (assumption).** With the spec's coefficients and real Open-Meteo weather, one cold snap (December 2022, 20+ freeze-thaw crossings in 14 days) multiplied hazard by about e^8 and left almost no potholes outside winter (12 in all Octobers). freeze_thaw_14d is capped at 6 and rain_then_frost_7d at 2 in config/synthetic.yaml; synthetic potholes still peak in February (Brighton-style late-winter spike) with about 350 a month in summer.
- **risk_latest is ranked by the real-label model (decision 17 Oct).** `roadsense.models.plan_risk` scores Southampton with the model trained on real FixMyStreet reports from three cities (`trained_on = real`). Two synthetic inputs remain, so `uses_synthetic = true`: (1) its history features for 2026 come from the SYNTHETIC council repair log, mapped by within-city rank onto the 2015-19 FixMyStreet count distribution because real 2020-26 reports are too thin (`history_source = synthetic`); (2) week-to-week movement is a ward-level weather multiplier from the synthetic-trained weekly model, which scales whole wards and never reorders segments within a ward. Expected yearly potholes are the real model's risk shares applied to Southampton's FOI 2025 total. The synthetic-trained weekly model's own output is kept as risk_synthetic_latest.parquet; the spec's single combined synthetic-plus-real model is not built.
- **Brighton test and leave-one-city-out not run yet (M5).** Lane A's Brighton and Portsmouth data landed at 5da0950; lane B runs them after Merge 1 (the code runs both automatically once two or more cities are in the labels).
- **Real-world check is small (M5).** On real segments, 38 of the 41 Southampton FixMyStreet reports from the last 12 months snap within 15 m; numbers in reports/risk_eval.md are noisy at that size.
- **Forward weather (M5).** Weeks 1 to 3 use lane A's Open-Meteo forecast rows; week 4 is beyond the 16-day forecast and uses 10-year weekly climatology (weather_basis = climatology_10y, uses_forecast = false).
- **Wear saturates.** Synthetic potholes average about one per segment every two years, so "time to first pothole" is short and wear is above 90% on many older segments. Pipeline-correct for the synthetic world, but worth recalibrating against a real repair log.
- **Legacy lamps barely survive into the fault log (M7).** Legacy units (20,000 h scale) burn out within about five years and are replaced with LEDs, so almost all legacy burnouts happen before the log starts in 2016. Only 11 legacy units are in the fit; the LED fit recovers the generator's 60,000 h / shape 3.
- **Streetlight model does not beat the install-year baseline (M7).** Every unit runs on the same burn-hour clock, so burn hours and install year rank lamps the same way; precision in the top 5% is within noise of the baseline (reports/lights_eval.md).
- **Brighton risk map (map only).** outputs/risk_brighton_latest.parquet comes from the real-label model with real FixMyStreet history (`history_source = real`, `uses_synthetic = false`). Brighton has no public yearly repair log, so the yearly total is ASSUMED as Southampton's FOI 2025 repairs per km times Brighton's road length (`scale_source`). There is no weekly weather model for Brighton, so its 4 weekly rows repeat the yearly risk and are not comparable with Southampton's weather-scaled weeks. No wear, lights or plan for Brighton.

## Building sites and utility digs (18 Oct)
- **No open planning applications or council-house pipelines.** planning.data.gov.uk's planning-application dataset has 0 records for Southampton and Brighton (checked 18 Oct 2026), and neither council publishes its council-house building programme as open data. The brownfield land register is the open list of sites with permission for homes; sites on publicly owned land are the closest open proxy for council or public housing.
- **Southampton's brownfield register is stale.** planning.data.gov.uk holds 37 Southampton sites, all entered in December 2017; the council's own register page sits behind an Imperva check and is not fetched. Southampton's current building sites come from OpenStreetMap `landuse=construction` only (31 in the city's road bounding box, 18 Oct 2026), which has no dates and so cannot be tested on past years.
- **A register site is a point.** It has no boundary or access road, so "near a building site" means within 150 m of the point (ASSUMPTION), and construction is assumed to run for up to 3 years after permission (planning permission lasts 3 years in England). The register does not say when building started or why a site left the register.
- **Register history has survivor bias.** Registers began in 2017; earlier permission dates only appear for sites still listed then, so older segment-years under-count sites.
- **Street Manager starts in July 2020.** Utility digs cannot be tested on Southampton (FixMyStreet labels stop in 2019); the held-out check is Brighton 2023-25 only, and the model learns the feature from Brighton 2022 rows.
- **Street Manager has permits, not reinstatement quality.** It says where a trench was opened and closed, the promoter and the activity type, not whether the reinstatement failed. Footway-only digs are counted separately from carriageway digs. A dig is linked to a segment when its works midpoint is within 20 m.
- **The DfT permit archive is about 1 GB a month** (all of England) and its zips have no end-of-central-directory record, so `roadsense.ingest.streetworks` walks the central directory itself, keeps only completed works (WORK_STOP events) for the two councils, and deletes each month after reading.
- **Both are risk-model inputs (team decision, 18 Oct), and on held-out years they do not help** (`reports/context_eval.md`). With both added, Brighton 2023-25 moves +0.6 points (-2.2 to +3.7) and Southampton 2018-19 -4.5 (-7.4 to -1.1): Southampton is clearly worse, and its model-vs-last-year gap now includes 0. Single sets swing either way with LightGBM column order, so the effects are small and unstable. Utility digs carry real signal (Brighton roads dug up the year before had 2.0x the report rate of roads in the same model-risk decile) but the model learns digs from Brighton 2022 rows alone. Switch: `risk.context_features` in `config/synthetic.yaml`. The page says "used in the risk model; no clear gain on held-out years yet" and reads this from `data/models/context_eval.json`.
- **Street Manager is monthly, not daily.** The daily refresh checks for a newly published month every day and appends it; DfT publishes each month after it ends, so digs lag by up to about six weeks. The model's dig features use whole calendar years (as in training), so new months change the road-card flags at once and the scores when the year turns.
- **OpenStreetMap construction sites are not model inputs** (no dates, so no earlier-years value); they appear on the map and road card only.
