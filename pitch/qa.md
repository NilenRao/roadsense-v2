# Judge Q&A: likely questions, short answers

Held-out numbers only: models trained on earlier years and scored on later years they never saw. Metric throughout:
**share of a year's real FixMyStreet pothole reports that land on the top 10% of road length** (chance = 10%).
Sources are files in this repo; `reports/risk_eval.md` is regenerated by `python -m roadsense.models.real_risk` and
`python -m roadsense.models.eval_ci`.

---

**1. Does it actually beat what a council does today?**
Clearly in Brighton, and probably in Southampton: on held-out years the model puts more of next year's reports on its top 10% of roads than ranking by last year's reports. The gain is about 10 points in Brighton and about 3 points in Southampton.
*Numbers:* Brighton 2023-25: 31.8% vs 21.7% (802 reports). Southampton 2018-19: 37.1% vs 33.7% (381 reports).
*Source:* `reports/risk_eval.md`, "Real-label evaluation", Southampton and Brighton test-year tables.

**2. Is that gap significant, or noise?**
In Brighton it holds up: resampling whole wards 1,000 times, the paired difference (model minus baseline) stays above zero. In Southampton the model is ahead in 94% of resamples but the 95% range just touches zero, so we say "ahead, not proven" there. We resample wards, not single roads, because reports cluster in space and single-road resampling would understate the noise.
*Numbers:* Brighton +10.1 points (95% +5.3 to +14.7), ahead in 100% of resamples; Southampton +3.4 points (95% -1.3 to +14.3), ahead in 94%. 1,000 resamples, seed 2026.
*Source:* `reports/risk_eval.md`, "Held-out ranges by ward", test-years table.

**3. Then why do the error bars overlap on the chart?**
Each bar's range is that method's own uncertainty, but the model and the baseline are scored on the same resampled wards, so they rise and fall together. The question is the gap between them in each resample: in Brighton it stays positive; in Southampton it is positive in 94% of resamples.
*Numbers:* Southampton model 37.1% (28.1 to 48.7), baseline 33.7% (23.1 to 40.1), paired difference -1.3 to +14.3 points. Brighton model 31.8% (28.7 to 35.8), baseline 21.7% (17.9 to 26.6), paired difference +5.3 to +14.7.
*Source:* `pitch/evidence_chart.png`, `reports/risk_eval.md` ("Held-out ranges by ward").

**4. Does it work this year?**
We can't tell yet: January to October 2026 has only 33 Southampton and 30 Brighton reports, too few for the result to mean anything. The model is ahead on both, but every range spans zero, so we don't claim it.
*Numbers:* Southampton 21% vs 12% (difference 95% -8 to +19 points); Brighton 30% vs 17% (0 to +35 points).
*Source:* `reports/risk_eval.md`, "Frozen 2026 check".

**5. What drives the ranking within a city?**
Within one city the order comes mainly from road length, reports on the road and its neighbours last year, bus routes, and (since 18 Oct) nearby building sites; the city itself and last year's weather move every road in an area together, so they barely reorder roads. Taking past reports out drops Southampton from 37.1% to 10.4%, so history is what carries the gain.
*Numbers:* top within-city features by mean |SHAP|: Southampton length 24%, neighbouring reports 7%, new homes permitted nearby 7%; Brighton length 24%, neighbouring reports 7%, bus routes 7%. Physical-only model: Southampton 10.4%, Brighton 21.2%.
*Source:* `reports/risk_eval.md`, "Top 5 features within each city" and the physical-only rows of the test-year tables.

**6. What is synthetic, and why?**
The council's own records (pothole repair log, resurfacing history, lamp register and lamp faults, crew inspection results) are not public, so we generate them from the real road map, traffic and weather, matched to Southampton's real yearly repair totals (FOI). The ranking model is trained on real FixMyStreet reports; in Southampton its 2026 history input comes from the synthetic repair log, because real reports there almost stop after 2019, and every screen badges this.
*Numbers:* yearly totals matched to FOI: 4,002 / 3,972 / 3,627 / 4,162 / 3,113 repairs (2021-25). Southampton FixMyStreet: none 2020-22.
*Source:* `config/synthetic.yaml`, `data/synthetic/_meta.json`, `DATA_GAPS.md`, `schemas.md` section 7 (`history_source`).

**7. Why don't you show weather as a reason?**
Last year's weather is in the model, but Open-Meteo gives only 2 to 6 distinct values across a city's wards, so it would show the same number for most roads and read as street-specific when it isn't. It shifts whole areas together and barely changes which roads come first; the weekly forecast still sets the timing in Southampton.
*Numbers:* 3 distinct values across Southampton's 17 wards; 6 across Brighton's 23.
*Source:* `roadsense/models/plan_risk.py` (`NEVER_A_REASON`), `reports/risk_eval.md` (note under "Top 5 features within each city").

**8. How is Brighton different from Southampton?**
Brighton runs on 100% real inputs: the same model, with real FixMyStreet history, giving an inspection list of open reports first, then the 50 roads ranked highest by predicted risk within Brighton. There is no wear model, plan or £ for Brighton, because those need the council's resurfacing and cost records.
*Numbers:* 55 rows: 5 roads with an open report in the last 120 days, then the top 50 by risk. Held-out: 31.8% vs 21.7%.
*Source:* `outputs/risk_brighton_latest.parquet` (`uses_synthetic = false`), `GET /plan/inspection?city=Brighton`, `schemas.md` section 17.

**9. What happens on a road, or in a city, with no report history?**
Without history the model falls back on physical features (length, traffic, buses, junctions, flood zone), and that is below the "last year's reports" baseline in Southampton and only level with it in Brighton. So we don't claim cold-start skill: a new city should load its own repair log first.
*Numbers:* physical-only vs baseline: Southampton 10.4% vs 33.7% (difference -23.3, 95% -29.3 to -13.0); Brighton 21.2% vs 21.7% (-0.5, 95% -7.0 to +3.6). Trained without a city: Brighton 19.5% vs 16.5%, Portsmouth 19.9% vs 13.1%, Southampton 34.8% vs 33.5% (ranges span zero).
*Source:* `reports/risk_eval.md`, physical-only rows and "leave-one-city-out" tables.

**10. What about privacy?**
We use only public FixMyStreet reports (location, date, category, title and status) and drop reporter names. Titles are redacted for house numbers, names and postcodes before they are shown, cached or sent to Gemini, and free-text descriptions never leave the git-ignored `data/raw/` folder.
*Numbers:* only public report text and synthetic data are sent to Gemini; no council data.
*Source:* `roadsense/prevent/redact_titles.py`, `.gitignore`, `schemas.md` (Privacy convention), `tests/test_lane_c_brighton.py` (redaction test).

**11. What does the AI cost?**
About £0.00056 per triaged report, so under £3 a year even if every one of Southampton's yearly pothole repairs arrived as a report. With hosting it's about £6.30 per officer per month, and the weekly plan itself uses no LLM.
*Numbers:* £1.74 to £2.32 a year at 3,113 to 4,162 repairs (upper bound); £6.30/officer/month with 4 officers and £25/month hosting (assumptions).
*Source:* `reports/cost.md` (measured token counts; prices and volumes are stated assumptions).

**12. Who approves the work orders?**
A highways officer does: Gemini only drafts a work order (category, segment, urgency from the risk model, recommended action), and nothing is dispatched until an officer presses Approve. Brighton's triage is read-only, with no work orders.
*Numbers:* draft → `pending_approval` → `approved` (with officer and time recorded).
*Source:* `roadsense/api/service.py` (`approve`), `POST /workorders/{id}/approve`, `roadsense/agent/triage.py`.

**13. Does it know when the council already resurfaced a road?**
Yes, for display: plan rows and road cards show "Treated <date>" when Southampton's own published scheme lists show carriageway work completed or in progress within 2 years before the plan date. It is a prompt to check before treating again; it does not change urgency or ranking. The lists come from the council's live 2026 page and Wayback copies of its removed 2023/24 pages, matched to roads by exact name and ward, plus six hand-checked matches for the Millbrook Flyover.
*Numbers:* 1 of the 50 plan rows is flagged: #38 Bassett Avenue, "Treated May 2026". The 2023/24 schemes (Millbrook Flyover £219,980, Winchester Road inlay) date to March 2024 at the latest, more than 2 years before 12 Oct 2026, so they are not flagged. Window fixed at 2 years on 18 Oct; don't change it before judging.
*Source:* `roadsense/api/treated.py`, `data/processed/soton_resurfacing.csv`, `data/resurfacing_manual_matches.csv`.

**14. Do building sites and utility digs help?**
Not on held-out years. They are in the model at the team's request, and roads dug up the year before did have twice the report rate of similar-risk roads in Brighton, but the model can only learn digs from 2022 (Street Manager starts in 2020) and Southampton's test years have no dig data. Adding both moved Brighton up 0.6 points and Southampton down 4.5.
*Numbers:* Brighton 31.8% with them vs 31.2% without (+0.6, 95% -2.2 to +3.7); Southampton 37.1% vs 41.6% (-4.5, 95% -7.4 to -1.1). Sources: brownfield land registers (planning.data.gov.uk), OpenStreetMap construction sites, DfT Street Manager, all open data.
*Source:* `reports/context_eval.md`, `DATA_GAPS.md` (building sites and utility digs).

**15. Is the data live, or a snapshot?**
Both: the repo carries a snapshot from the event, and a daily refresh updates it unattended. Once a day it pulls new FixMyStreet reports (and rechecks which are still open), the weather forecast, building sites and any newly published Street Manager month, then re-scores with the frozen models (no retraining). Each run builds into a new folder and only switches the app over after validation; on any failure the previous data stays live. The header shows "Data updated" and turns amber STALE after 36 hours.
*Numbers:* 120-day window for "open" reports; last 7 builds kept; Street Manager is published monthly, so digs move once a month. Southampton's refresh stops after the next Monday until the synthetic repair log is regenerated; Brighton (all real) keeps refreshing.
*Source:* README "Daily refresh", `roadsense/refresh/run.py`, `scripts/schedule_refresh.bat`.
