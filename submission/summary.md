# 150-word summary (submission form)

Southampton fills about 4,000 potholes a year, and in four of the last five years spent nothing on preventative
maintenance. A reactive repair costs £87.80, a planned one £56.94.

RoadSense helps Southampton's highways officers decide which roads to treat this week, before potholes form. A model
trained on real FixMyStreet reports from three south-coast cities scores every council road using traffic counts, bus
routes, flood zones, building sites, utility digs, freeze-thaw forecasts and pothole history, refreshed daily. It
explains each score, shows which roads are worth treating, and triages residents' reports into work orders an officer
approves. On years it never saw, its top tenth of Brighton's roads caught 32% of reported potholes against 22% for
last year's reports; in Southampton, 37% against 34%.

Councils or their contractors pay an annual licence. Every input is national open data, so another city needs only its
boundary and repair records.
