## 150-word summary (submission)
Southampton repaired 3,000 to 4,000 potholes a year from 2021 to 2025, and 71% of its drivers strongly agree potholes
are a local problem, the highest share in the country. In January 2026 the government rated Southampton amber for
road maintenance, while Portsmouth, which maintains its roads proactively, was rated green. Planned repairs cost
£56.94 on average against £87.80 for reactive ones.

RoadSense helps the highways officers who plan Southampton's weekly maintenance decide which streets to treat before
potholes form. It scores every road segment using heavy-vehicle load from DfT counts, bus routes, flood areas and
freeze-thaw weather, explains each score, and produces a weekly prevention list for an officer to approve.

The council or its contractor pays an annual licence. Every input is national open data, so a second city needs only
its boundary and its own repair records.

## Honesty lines to keep in the pitch (judges reward these)
- Southampton's real FixMyStreet reports stop after 2019 (residents moved to the council's own system), so the model is
  tested on Southampton 2018–19 and Brighton 2023–25, plus a frozen-model check on Jan–Oct 2026.
- Council repair logs aren't public, so they're replaced by clearly labelled SYNTHETIC data. Accuracy on synthetic data
  is a pipeline check, not proof; the real-data tests are the proof.
- Complaint counts measure who reports, not where the damage is: the report channel change in 2020 shows how fragile
  complaint-driven planning is.
